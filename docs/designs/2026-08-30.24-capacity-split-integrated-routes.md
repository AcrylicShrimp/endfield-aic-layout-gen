# Capacity-Split Integrated Routes

## Problem

Facility-instance wiring expresses exact material flow between endpoints. One logical edge may exceed the capacity of one belt or pipe because facility work capacity and transport-line capacity are independent. Rejecting that edge prevents otherwise valid layouts even when the source and target expose enough compatible ports for parallel lines.

## Preparation Contract

The integrated-layout preparation stage receives validated instance wiring, item transport kinds, and transport capacities. Before creating solver routes, it divides each positive-rate logical edge into the minimum number of parallel route inputs whose individual rates do not exceed the selected transport capacity.

For an edge rate `R` and capacity `C`, preparation emits `ceil(R / C)` routes. Every route except possibly the last carries `C`; the final route carries the exact positive remainder. The sum of emitted rates must equal `R` exactly.

All emitted routes preserve the original source, target, item, kind, and transport layer. Exact rational arithmetic is mandatory.

## Solver Behavior

Each parallel route independently selects compatible source and target ports and occupies grid cells on its transport layer. Existing route-cell exclusion prevents two routes on the same layer from sharing a cell, including a facility port connection cell. Therefore insufficient compatible ports or routing space produces an ordinary infeasible result rather than an input-validation error.

Belt and pipe routes remain separate layers and may overlap each other under the existing rule.

## Diagnostics

Missing endpoints, missing item definitions, unrepresentable transport capacities, and incompatible port kinds remain invalid-input diagnostics. A logical rate above one line's capacity is no longer invalid input.

## Verification

Tests must prove that a rate of one item per second becomes two half-item-per-second belt routes when belt capacity is one item per two seconds. Existing single-route and belt/pipe layer behavior must remain unchanged.
