# Success-adaptive route frontier training

## Status and question

This experiment is implemented and evaluated. The training hypothesis failed:
focused collection produced 47 unique depth-one successes, but zero of three
seeds transferred them to fresh-root greedy depth-one behavior. The complete
result is recorded in
[`2026-09-20-success-adaptive-route-frontier-training.md`](../reports/2026-09-20-success-adaptive-route-frontier-training.md).

This was the accepted training experiment after the frozen-policy frontier probe.
It implements one complete exploration tooth at the first crossed continuation:
discover diverse depth-one routes from policy-owned actionable prefixes,
consolidate them, and judge mastery only from fresh-root greedy evaluation.

The falsifiable question is: **does reallocating a fixed 64-session update
budget toward the currently failing crossed depth-one frontier produce fresh-root
greedy depth-one success in at least two of three initialization seeds?**

The retained route replay runs remain the control. The treatment keeps their
model, PPO/SIL objective, reward, native session, task generator, replay bounds,
32 updates and evaluation panel. Only collection allocation and the exact
temperature behavior distribution change.

## Policy-owned frontier

Normal root sessions still begin from externally configured facility placement
and route every connection through the learned policy. When such a session
commits route zero in an exact two-connection crossed task, the collector may
offer the resulting state to a bounded frontier buffer.

Admission requires all of the following:

- every prefix command came from the current policy in the ordinary session;
- replaying those commands through a fresh native session reproduces exactly one
  committed route and no provisional route;
- the actual next route exposes a nonempty local action domain;
- the semantic geometry-and-command digest is new.

No path solver, demonstration, automatic repair, alternate route ordering or
global feasibility test participates. A prefix that locally blocks the next
route is counted and rejected. The buffer retains at most 32 prefixes and draws
them round-robin so one lucky route cannot monopolize practice.

Candidates collected in update `u` become eligible only after all PPO/SIL epochs
for `u` finish. Frontier sessions in update `u+1` rebuild the native state by
replaying the admitted commands, then collect only the depth-one route. These
new depth-one choices are on-policy samples from the current frozen rollout
policy; the historical prefix only defines the initial-state curriculum.

## Exact temperature likelihood

Each conditional factor uses

```text
q_T(a) = exp(log(p(a)) / T) / sum_b exp(log(p(b)) / T)
```

where `p` is the model's masked factor distribution. Every collected choice
stores `T`, its joint log probability under `q_T`, and its entropy under `q_T`.
PPO recomputes the numerator through the same transform, including when a
minibatch mixes root samples at `T=1` and frontier samples at another
temperature. Temperature is fixed for the complete collection and all PPO
epochs consuming it. SIL remains a consolidation objective under the base
`T=1` policy, independent of the temperature that originally discovered an
archived successful action.

## Controller

The controller observes only targeted frontier attempts and successful
depth-one command identities. It has three phases:

1. `discover`: temperature 1.5 until eight semantically unique depth-one
   successes have been observed;
2. `consolidate`: temperature 1.0 until fresh-root greedy evaluation records at
   least four crossed depth-one successes;
3. `master`: temperature 0.5 and the minimum frontier allocation, preserving a
   small rehearsal stream while fresh-root evaluation remains authoritative.

This is one sawtooth: exploration rises at the new boundary, falls after diverse
success, and falls again after transfer to fresh-root greedy behavior. A later
frontier would begin a new tooth; this experiment does not open depth two.

Let `s` successes occur in `n` targeted attempts. The controller calculates the
95% Wilson lower bound `p_L`. In discovery, `r` is the remaining unique-success
target; in consolidation, `r=2`. The requested frontier budget is

```text
B = clamp(8, 32, ceil(r / max(p_L, 0.02)))
```

Zero success therefore remains bounded at 32 attempts per update. The treatment
uses `B` frontier sessions and `64-B` fresh-root sessions. If no prefix is
available, all 64 sessions remain fresh-root. This keeps the declared session
budget fixed while exposing the changed option and command counts in metrics.

## Metrics and decision

Every update reports controller phase, temperature, requested and actual
frontier sessions, prefix admissions/rejections, cumulative targeted attempts,
raw and unique successes, Wilson bounds and fresh-root greedy crossed depth-one
successes. Route summaries add the joint shape-by-roll-in-depth table so
aggregate aligned success cannot hide the target stratum.

The final comparison uses seeds 101, 202 and 303. The treatment passes only if:

- at least two seeds produce a fresh-root greedy `crossed-depth-1` success;
- the success comes from ordinary root evaluation, not restored prefixes;
- native validation accepts every reported route;
- no temperature-likelihood mismatch or nonfinite PPO metric occurs.

A stochastic or replay-only improvement does not pass. If the treatment creates
frontier successes but no fresh-root transfer, the next change should address
consolidation or root-state supply. If it creates no frontier successes despite
actionable prefixes, revisit the controller budget or optimization. A
representation comparison is justified only if the controlled frontier itself
again fails.
