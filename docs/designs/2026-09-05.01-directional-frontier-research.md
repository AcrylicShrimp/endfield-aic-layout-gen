# Directional frontier construction and bounded joint repair

## Status and decision

Research plan following Q0 (`7bd21e0`) and the user's directional-growth proposal on 2026-09-05.
The offline R0 audit is complete; directional production behavior is not implemented or adopted.
This extends, rather than replaces, the [accepted constructive planner](2026-09-02.00-constructive-layout-planner.md).

Hypothesis: organize growth around remaining material interfaces, with a preferred global flow
direction. Facilities whose incident requirements are already covered need no reserved *new facility
port* solely for unused catalog ports. Facilities and shared networks with remaining demands should
keep usable expansion interfaces toward the corresponding upstream/downstream side. This may avoid
the future-boundary traps observed in Q0 without abandoning compact placement.

The direction is a candidate-generation heuristic, not a game constraint, fixed canvas, strict
layer assignment, or proof of future completion. Retain immediate routing and independent validation.
No global place-everything-then-route phase, rigid module interface, or exact-solver fallback.

## Observations versus hypotheses

See [R0 evidence](../benchmarks/constructive-directional-frontier/report.md).

- The complete baseline has facility owners at both horizontal extremes. Route-only improvement
  cannot reduce its width while those placements stay fixed.
- The guarded N=4 partial state has 27 facilities: 20 incident-closed, five input-only active,
  and two active in both directions. Nine incident requirements remain, all projected as new-port
  attachments. Every side of its used rectangle is owned by transport geometry.
- The recorded rejected continuation connects another moss-powder input but blocks the target's
  final-output boundary. The accepted prefix remains valid; the rejected trial is not committed.
- These are observations. They do not establish that all legal attachment alternatives are exhausted,
  that every perimeter route is an obstacle, or that global direction alone solves the blockage.

## State distinctions

Compute remaining requirements from immutable original wiring minus audited covered IDs. Include
external supply and final output from the beginning, even if physical external completion is later.
Include unresolved edges whose two facilities are already placed. Reconstructing a network must never
rewrite the requirement ledger to make its own output appear complete.

For each placed facility track remaining incoming and outgoing requirements:

- `incident_closed`: neither remains; unused catalog ports alone do not keep it active.
- `input_open`: only incoming requirements remain.
- `output_open`: only outgoing requirements remain.
- `both_open`: both remain; do not treat the facility as a single-sided leaf.

These are obligation states, not geometric labels. An incident-closed facility may still feed a
shared trunk needed by an unplaced consumer. Network expansion therefore has its own state and
capacity-aware attachment options. Do not freeze or bury that trunk merely because its originating
facility is incident-closed. Likewise, incident closure does not certify movable geometry.

The growth frontier is a set of active interfaces, not necessarily the outermost row of buildings.
Several separated interfaces and lateral branches can remain active. A global direction supplies
a preferred upstream/downstream half-plane; it does not require a perfectly straight boundary.

## R1 input and output contract, before implementation

Inputs:

- Immutable current valid node and original wiring ledger, runtime catalogs and current SCC group.
- Explicit preferred flow axis (`east`, `west`, `north`, `south`) for the experiment. Disabled is
  the existing behavior. Do not infer a game limit or silently rotate catalogs.
- Existing nearby shortlist/success limits and placement/A* budgets. Any new work cap has its own
  reported unit and includes rejected trials. A rollout or repair budget must not be hidden here.

Internal diagnostic DTOs:

```text
FrontierDemand
  original_requirement_ids, inside_instance, direction, item, transport, exact_rate
  proposed_new_port_options, proposed_existing_network_options
  preferred_expansion_side, option_status

FrontierFacilityState
  instance, remaining_input_ids, remaining_output_ids, incident_state

DirectionalPlacementDecision
  growth_group, axis, parent_node_id, selected_pose
  generated/shortlisted/attempted/valid candidate counts by preferred/escape class
  first_score, selected_score, rejected_reason_counts, consumed_work, termination
```

Demand grouping must respect jointly assignable compatible ports and transport capacity. Multiple
logical requirements may share an interface; different commodities cannot be merged just to fit.
Existing-network capacity options sharing a segment must not each spend its residual independently.
Before adding persistent reservations, define their precise cells/channels, option-selection
lifetime, and invalidation when geometry changes. Individually accessible options are not a joint
completion certificate. Unsupported or unmeasured joint feasibility is explicitly `unknown`.

Output: either one immediately routed, independently validated growth transaction, or the same
last valid parent plus a structured local failure. Atomic SCC members remain private until the
entire group validates. No duplicate facility instances or new requirements.

Diagnostics have stable code, severity, stage (`directional-frontier`), message, entity and consumed
bound. Proposed codes: `directional-preference-unavailable` (info, use escape candidates),
`directional-candidates-exhausted` (warning, last valid node retained),
`directional-joint-interface-unknown` (info, not proof), and
`directional-future-boundary-blocked` (warning, identify requirement and rejected candidate).

## R1 algorithm and controlled experiments

Start with candidate ordering, not permanent spatial reservations:

1. Preserve the current frontier order, pipe priority, SCC membership and connection routing.
2. For the same new facility/group, classify legal poses by the upstream/downstream relationship
   to the connected anchor under the selected flow axis. An upstream supplier prefers the upstream
   half-plane; a newly introduced consumer prefers downstream. Try lateral branches as alternatives.
3. Within those poses, favor legal rotations whose *required* interface options point toward the
   corresponding expansion side. Port normals and material-flow vectors are not identical:
   an upstream-facing input receives downstream-moving material.
4. Keep alternatives when several directions are needed. For example, the target catalog has belt
   inputs on south/east, pipe input on west and belt outputs on north in rotation zero. No rotation
   turns all required inputs into one common face. Do not reject such a facility as impossible.
5. Reuse the existing collision, corridor, route, future-boundary and original-wiring validation.
   Count and bound all trials. Choose among valid candidates for the same coverage using the
   unchanged actual `(area, physical transport tiles, turns)` score. Direction affects proposals,
   not the official objective or validity.
6. Preserve documented nonpreferred escape candidates. Once a changed prefix gets trapped, simply
   enabling coarse poses is not guaranteed to finish it. Report failure honestly.

R1a ablation: identical nearby pose pool, score, success count, frontier order and routing; only
shortlist ordering changes. Compare disabled and each of the four axes, starting with N=4 for the
recorded failure. Also retain the old complete default. Count the trials spent on axis selection;
four runs are not the same cost as one. In axis runs do not separately search for a favorable seed
rotation without charging and documenting that changed variable.

R1b is conditional, not bundled into R1a: if ordering merely moves the same boundary trap, measure
directional interface protection against R1a, with explicit joint options and reservation lifetime.
An abstract direction does not keep a route from crossing an active exit. Conversely, reserving an
infinite half-plane or every optional port would needlessly recreate a dispersed constructor.

Fixtures: chain; split/shared producer; converging demands; both-open facility; mixed belt/pipe
ports on different faces; atomic seed cycle; existing-network attachment with shared capacity;
external output held open while upstream growth continues; actual Heavy Xiranite request.

Acceptance requires 59 facilities, 89 internal and seven external requirements for the unchanged
Heavy Xiranite input, final independent validation and original-ID/rate audit. Those numbers are
workload observations, not architecture constants. Record full score only for complete results;
partial coverage and the failing requirement are separate outputs. Use equal-prefix/same-added-group
comparisons for local lookahead; do not rank unequal covered subgraphs by raw partial area.

Measure release construction and file output separately, placement trials, port bundles, actual
route transactions, A* searches and expanded states, boundary checks, validator calls, and bounded
memory. Add missing counters before claiming equal-work superiority. Time repeats measure jitter;
axis/seed/order variants measure distinct strategies. HTML and the existing tailnet site stay unchanged.

## R2: bounded joint repair if required

Use measured failed-interface or boundary-owner evidence to choose the mutable neighborhood.
Include moved facilities, their connections, and spatially blocking route pieces even when unrelated
to the moved facilities. Start with a small recent-growth transaction; for complete-layout improvement
start with one outer strip. Expand the neighborhood only under an explicit work/resource budget.

Cut interfaces must carry channel identity, coordinate, direction, commodity, transport, exact flow,
and outside connectivity. Initially freeze boundary flows so outside loads remain valid. Cut through
complete component interfaces, or include the entire component. An outside cut anchor left in the
removed strip defeats compaction. Include legal external-terminal relocation when it owns that strip.

Retry local poses, ports and the existing route engine's repair order in private scratch state.
Keep unrelated validated geometry; integrate only after independent validation and original-wiring
audit. Construction repair must restore valid covered growth; complete-layout improvement must also
improve the official score. Local exhaustion is not global infeasibility. Freeze/residual contracts
must be specified and tested before implementing a local oracle or arbitrary network resynthesis.

Always preserve the existing complete baseline artifact. If a new construction strategy fails and
the product returns that baseline, report `candidate_complete: false` separately from fallback
availability. Never count fallback completion as evidence that the new strategy completed.

## Next decision

Implement and measure R1a as the next isolated milestone. If it completes and improves the full
factory within a useful budget, consider adoption; if it fails, report the exact changed obstruction.
Use that result to choose interface protection (R1b), same-prefix continuation tests, or joint repair
(R2). Do not jump directly to a broad hierarchy or a replacement global solver.
