# Optimized PPO device and precision measurements

CUDA FP32 is the provisional learner choice for the action-only actor workload.
The FP32 device comparison completed before the separate AMP comparison. Both
used the registered seed-17 batch-8 checkpoint, its first shuffled 64 selections,
nonempty Adam states and the optimized model execution. All cells reset weights
and Adam state before each measurement. GPU timers synchronize at boundaries.

| FP32 device / microbatch | Preparation + transfer | Resident learning | Combined |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| CPU / 8 | 0.0320 s | 0.3658 s | 0.3988 s |
| CPU / 64 | 0.0375 s | 0.3770 s | 0.4146 s |
| CUDA / 8 | 0.0398 s | 0.0601 s | 0.1067 s |
| CUDA / 32 | 0.0471 s | 0.0401 s | 0.0953 s |
| CUDA / 64 | 0.0418 s | 0.0353 s | 0.0846 s |

Each number is the median of three reset timings after warmup. Resident timings
are separate executions; their medians need not sum to the combined median.
The combined CUDA/64 measurement was 4.7 times faster than CPU/8. This is a
learner minibatch comparison, not full factory-generation/collection throughput.
CPU preparation constructs complete tensors before transfer, avoiding dynamic
candidate filtering on the GPU. Normal training and the probe share prepared
minibatch execution. The device comparison took 24.52 seconds including reference
workload preparation and diagnostic captures.

| Precision, CUDA / 64 | Combined | Resident learning | Max initial log-probability drift | Max initial value drift |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| FP32 | 0.0685 s | 0.0247 s | 9.54e-7 | 7.45e-8 |
| BF16 autocast | 0.0721 s | 0.0284 s | 0.005795 | 0.001178 |
| FP16 autocast + GradScaler | 0.0806 s | 0.0371 s | 0.000621 | 0.000075 |

The AMP comparison took 23.07 seconds and included its own CPU reference.
All cells passed the declared engineering numeric thresholds and actually
stepped both optimizers. BF16 actor/critic clipped-gradient relative L2 errors
were 0.00874/0.02567; FP16 errors were 0.00115/0.00247. Their cosines exceeded
0.9999. AMP did not improve this workload's median time, so FP32 remains the
provisional choice. The GPU was an RTX 5090 with Torch 2.14.0+cu130, CUDA 13.0,
one CPU Torch thread, deterministic algorithms, and IEEE FP32 matmul/convolution
settings rather than TF32. CUDA/64 peak allocated memory was about 0.70 GB in
FP32 and 0.62 GB under autocast for this workload.

The repeated FP32 cell varied between the two phases (0.0846 versus 0.0685 s),
so small differences should not be treated as precise universal speed ratios.
These short kernel measurements do not prove equal learned performance. They
also do not characterize richer actor inputs: the user's replacement objective
requires extending and testing the twelve input contracts and checking
representative richer-input throughput before finalizing the screen setup.
