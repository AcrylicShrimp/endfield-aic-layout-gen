## Update-boundary measurements

Each exact categorical KL mean covers all 1024 collected states, excluding padded
candidates. Here the reference is the **incoming policy for that update**, not the
original checkpoint. These distances cannot be added to obtain cumulative drift.

| Distance | Minimum across updates | Median | Maximum |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Incoming to post-optimizer | 0.0305676 | 0.0648548 | 0.1707294 |
| Incoming to post-calibration | 0.0230669 | 0.0531470 | 0.1478446 |
| Post-optimizer to post-calibration | 0.0011465 | 0.0033697 | 0.0130342 |

Calibration reduces distance from the incoming policy in all 32 updates. This is
not proof that it improves assessment outcomes, but it does not support calibration
alone as the cause of the observed policy movement. At update 24 the incoming-to-
final KL is 0.0926513. The largest value, 0.1478446, occurs at update 25, after the
worst measured assessment and before later recovery. A high KL is therefore not by
itself evidence that an individual update is harmful.

The evidence supports testing a bounded reduction in update size with a declared
matched continuation, including both reliability and area quality. It does not
justify permanently lowering the learning rate, capping an arbitrary KL threshold,
or launching another full capacity matrix without a distinct comparison. The
original six-stream `not_met` result and all unobserved held-out gates remain intact.
Stage 4 and the original stage-5 target remain incomplete; no fundamental
contradiction has been established.
