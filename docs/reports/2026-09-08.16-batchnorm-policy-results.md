# Spatial BatchNorm in short-mastery PPO

Status: complete. All three runs and audits passed. BN improved B's final mean area
reduction and success consistency; A remained weak and did not improve on average.

The user requested BatchNorm and accepted ordinary floating-point training variation.
This bounded follow-up compares three BatchNorm policies with the completed three
no-normalization short-mastery policies in the
[preceding study](2026-09-08.15-mastery-curriculum-results.md).

## Controlled change

The [contract](../designs/2026-09-08.08-batch-normalized-policy.md) selects affine BN
after each of three convolutions and before ReLU. Model outputs and the variable
candidate set receive no BN. Real raster cells contribute to statistics; padding
does not. Common network parameters retain their seeded initialization, and BN adds
160 trainable scale/offset parameters plus running-statistics buffers.

This implementation holds running statistics fixed during collection and PPO gradient
updates. After each update it makes one training-only calibration pass through the
1,024 collected observations in 16 batches, updating EMA statistics with momentum 0.1.
The next actor snapshot includes those buffers. This is a frozen-statistics PPO
variant, not conventional training-mode BN on every minibatch. Calibration introduces
an additional policy change outside the clipped PPO objective; the comparison includes
that lifecycle choice. No QAT, deterministic-algorithm requirement, reward shaping,
new actions, changed critic initialization or changed advancement criteria was added.

All runs use eight-edit training episodes, eight concurrent environments, seeds
17/29/43, 65,536 sampled edits, 64 PPO updates, four epochs and 64-example minibatches.
Original-root outcome evaluation remains 128 edits and 32 seeds per case. Both cases
must reach 52/64 successes in two consecutive eight-edit assessments before advancement.
The declared protocol is `tools/factory-edit-pilot/mastery-batchnorm-study.json`.

The baseline training source is `6e1a606` and BN training source is `97e9d04`.
Baseline source is deliberately retained as the active experimental control. Results
are descriptive across three trained policies, not a universal claim about BN.

observed B objective. The best A root score remains `(147, 24, 0)` from `(154, 25, 0)`.

scripted starts can be better (A reaches area 133); these are not root discoveries.

## Curriculum progression

Remaining-one passes at the same sampled-edit checkpoints with or without BN:
32,768 / 24,576 / 32,768 for seeds 17 / 29 / 43.

| BN seed | Remaining-two result | Final assessed level | Final A/B assessment successes |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| 17 | Passes at 49,152 | 3 | 1/64, 64/64 |
| 29 | Passes at 49,152 | 3 | 0/64, 64/64 |
| 43 | One pass at 57,344, then fails | 2 | 29/64, 64/64 |

Without BN, only seed 29 passes remaining-two, at the final 65,536 checkpoint;
none of the controls train at remaining-three. With BN, seeds 17 and 29 get 16,384
sampled edits each at remaining-three. Neither passes that level because A fails.
Seed 43 regresses on A after one passing assessment and correctly stays at level two.
The stage number is not the same geometric distance for both cases: A's first
scripted gain needs six edits, while B's needs three. At remaining-three, A still
has three preparation edits, but B has an empty prefix and is already at its original
root. Seeds 17 and 29 therefore each train on 8,192 original-root B edits (1,024
episodes); seed 43 has no original-root training. B results in the first two seeds
combine curriculum transfer and direct root training. Seed 43 demonstrates transfer
from edited starts. None establishes unseen-factory generalization.

Correction during roadmap review: the initial report incorrectly equated original-root
training with scheduler level zero. Root exposure is defined by an empty actual prefix,
including B at level three. `mastery_compare.py` now counts positive root training
episodes on that basis; all learning runs, outcome metrics and checkpoint evidence
remain unchanged. The original comparison artifact retains its provenance; its
`positive_root_training_episodes` field undercounts these two BN runs.

## Costs, checks and interpretation

| Seed | No-BN training seconds | BN training seconds | Included BN calibration seconds |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 17 | 27.801 | 28.993 | 2.325 |
| 29 | 26.948 | 29.065 | 2.311 |
| 43 | 27.367 | 29.465 | 2.350 |
| Mean | 27.372 | 29.174 | 2.329 |

Mean training time increases 6.5852%. These are descriptive end-to-end timings,
including setup/calibration and excluding assessment/outcome evaluation. Calibration
cost alone should not be added to the baseline time: learned trajectories and stage
exposure also change collection/update costs. The complete BN matrix with evaluations
and audits takes 215.254 seconds. Hardware and native release library match the baseline.

Totals: 196,608 sampled edits, 24,576 training episodes, 68,608 training setup edits,
192 PPO updates and 12,288 Adam steps. Each update calibrates once over 1,024 collected
observations; total calibration exposure is 196,608 observations in 3,072 batches.
Every BN layer's final saved batch counter is 1,024. Calibration consumes no extra
environment actions and no evaluation data. Root evaluation uses 172,032 edits;
assessment uses 24,576 plus an equal number in its independent replay. All 27 policy,
optimizer, RNG and normalization-state checkpoints remain retained.

All 58 Python tests pass on the server. Locally 55 pass, with two CUDA and one Linux
compiled-CPU skip. Workspace check/tests and the clean-source CLI smoke pass. Tests
cover packed-valid-cell BN equivalence, padding exclusion, frozen likelihood/statistics,
shared parameter initialization and actor-buffer transfer. The full audits verify
normalization calibration counts and stored buffers, sample accounting, source and
checkpoint consistency, all assessments against the Python reference, and accepted
root outputs/selected traces. No extra numerical-determinism investigation was run.

This small comparison supports using BN as a promising spatial-policy option for
further work, particularly for reliable B improvement. It does not support the claim
that BN always improves a model: A's mean declines slightly, one B seed loses average
gain, and remaining-three A is now the clearest exposed curriculum blocker. The
normalization lifecycle is part of this result. Further experiments should preserve
the successful B behavior while investigating A's stage transition; no additional
training or parameter search is included in this slice.
