# Short episodes and mastery curriculum results

Status: complete. All twelve runs and independent audits passed. Short episodes with
mastery advancement established easy-start learning and transferred to original-root
improvement in two of three training seeds; the other three conditions did not.

## Question and controls

The preceding [failure analysis](2026-09-08.14-curriculum-failure-analysis.md)
identified scarce easy-start exposure and advancement before reliable success. This
study crosses training horizons of 128 and 8 edits with fixed and mastery-based
advancement. The [frozen contract](../designs/2026-09-08.07-mastery-curriculum.md)
and `tools/factory-edit-pilot/mastery-study.json` specify the complete protocol.
Training source: `6e1a606cc599d26cbdc313fd78eb31c7037daa60`.

Each condition uses training seeds 17, 29 and 43, two battery cases, 65,536 sampled
edits, eight concurrent environments, 1,024 samples per PPO update, four epochs and
64-example minibatches. There are 64 PPO updates and 4,096 Adam steps per run.
Policies start identically within each seed. This is not a promise of bitwise-identical
training: deterministic CUDA algorithms were not enabled. Matched long fixed/mastery
runs have identical first-rollout episode records, but their first PPO update metrics
already differ numerically; the first episode-record differences occur at episodes
22, 25 and 17 for seeds 17, 29 and 43, before scheduler divergence. Treat the three
trained policies as the replication units, not paired deterministic trajectories.
The CUDA learner, model, action domain,
reward, critic initialization and original valid base remain unchanged.

Short episodes provide 8,192 resets versus 512 per run at the same sample budget.
This deliberately changes retry exposure and terminal GAE boundaries; it does not
isolate either effect and is not an equal-reset or equal-wall-time comparison.
Scripted start preparation is excluded from sampled training edits but counted
separately, with its execution time included in training time.

Fixed runs spend 8,192 sampled edits at each of remaining-one, remaining-two and
remaining-three, then 40,960 at original roots. Adaptive runs advance only after
at least 52/64 successes on **each** case in two consecutive assessments. Assessments
occur every 8,192 sampled edits, use eight-edit episodes and seeds 200000–200063,
and never enter PPO. They provide curriculum feedback, not independent outcome
validation. Root outcome evaluation always uses 128 edits and 32 seeds per case,
at 0, 8192, 16384, 24576, 32768, 49152 and 65536 sampled edits.

## Learning progression

| Adaptive condition / seed | Remaining-one passed at sampled edits | Remaining-two passed | Final assessment A/B | Next level / pass streak |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| Long / 17 | 57,344 | No | 1/64, 0/64 at level two | 2 / 0 |
| Long / 29 | 65,536 | No training exposure | 64/64, 64/64 at level one | 2 / 0 |
| Long / 43 | Not passed | Not reached | 59/64, 12/64 at level one | 1 / 0 |
| Short / 17 | 32,768 | No | 64/64, 0/64 at level two | 2 / 0 |
| Short / 29 | 24,576 | 65,536 | 64/64, 64/64 at level two | 3 / 0 |
| Short / 43 | 32,768 | One passing assessment only | 63/64, 63/64 at level two | 2 / 1 |

All three short adaptive runs passed level one; two long adaptive runs did. Passing
at the final checkpoint changes the saved controller but provides no training in the
new level. No adaptive run trained at level three or at original roots. Therefore
the positive root outcomes demonstrate transfer from edited starts on these same two
cases, not successful root-start training or generalization to unseen factories.
The scripted setup still provides privileged geometry, while its actions never enter
PPO or receive policy-gradient credit.

Neither fixed condition met the joint mastery threshold in any recorded assessment.
The short fixed runs nevertheless collected some level-two and level-three rewards,
mostly on A; these did not survive as root improvement. Positive training episodes
across all three seeds were 10 (long fixed), 265 (short fixed), 432 (long mastery)
and 12,251 (short mastery). These totals mix levels and unequal reset exposure and
must not be treated as comparable success probabilities.

The next stage is not uniformly a B-only blocker: short seed 17 has final level-two
B success 0/64, seed 29 passes both cases, and seed 43 temporarily loses A success
before reaching 63/64 on both. Stage transition remains fragile and seed-dependent.
The evidence supports continuing the short/mastery direction, while it does not
identify a unique optimization defect or prove that a larger budget will fix every run.
