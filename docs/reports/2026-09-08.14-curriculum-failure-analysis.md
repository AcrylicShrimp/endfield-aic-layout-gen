# Why the first curriculum did not produce reliable root improvement

Status: complete diagnosis. No training configuration, reward, action domain or model
weights were changed. This follow-up reads the frozen 12-run study and reconstructs
six initial CPU rollouts; it does not claim a complete causal ablation of all factors.

The strongest direct finding is that the scheduled curriculum advanced before its
one-step exercise was mastered. Most initial choices immediately left that exercise,
and reward-free PPO updates continued after the rare successes. Increasing episode
length alone does not address these observed failures.

## The effective curriculum exposure was small

Episodes contain 128 sampled edits. The remaining-one stage contains 8,192 edits:
64 episodes total, only 32 fresh starts per case. The agent receives an easy start
once per episode, not once per edit. At 1,024 edits per episode and the same total
allowance, that would fall to four starts per case. Longer horizons may help tasks
that actually require long sequences; this study has no evidence that 128 steps was
too short to finish its one-action exercise.

We reconstructed the first eight curriculum-serial episodes for each of three seeds.
The generated episode returns and complete episode counters matched the recorded runs.
After each first sampled action, the native diagnostic exhaustively tried every
candidate in the *next* actual action domain from the same restored state:

| First-action consequence | Episodes out of 24 |
| --- | ---: |
| Immediate positive reward | 1 |
| No reward, but a next action can still improve | 1 |
| No next action can produce positive reward | 22 |

The last row proves only absence of a one-step improvement in that precise state. It
is not a proof of irrecoverability or global infeasibility. Nevertheless, 22 initial
choices turned the planned one-step exercise into a harder task immediately, leaving
127 actions to wander or attempt repair. Most were erasures or terminal unbindings;
one was discard. `DISCARD_EDIT` returns to the valid base, which initially remains the
original root, not the unfinished curriculum draft.

Across the full study, all 15 positive training episodes occurred in remaining-one
training. Remaining-two and remaining-three produced none. The setup prefix itself
is excluded from PPO, so reward from completing a one-step start cannot teach the
prefix's earlier unbinding/erasure choices. Those preceding choices were supposed to
be learned in later stages; the experiment did not obtain positive experience there.

## The policies had not mastered the easy action

The checkpoint probe reads all 72 frozen policies on original, remaining-one,
remaining-two and remaining-three states for both cases (576 state-policy probes).
For remaining-one, the known immediate-success action is the sole bind among 37
candidates for A and 87 for B. Initial probabilities are almost uniform, about 2.70%
and 1.15%. At the first curriculum boundary, none of the 12 curriculum policy/case
combinations assigns that successful action more than 6.40% probability.

Representative exact frozen-policy probabilities:

| Condition / training seed / case | Initial | At 8,192 | Final 65,536 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Curriculum concurrent / 29 / A | 2.703% | 6.398% | 1.442% |
| Curriculum serial / 29 / B | 1.149% | 2.650% | 0.925% |
| Curriculum serial / 43 / A | 2.704% | 3.871% | 1.530% |

The first row's successful bind ranked first at 8,192. Thus some useful preference
was learned, but the stochastic training/evaluation policy still usually chose other
actions. The subsequent probability decline is directly measured; it does not prove
that one particular optimizer term caused the decline. Other policy/case combinations
show different trajectories, all retained in the raw output.

The prior report's isolated original-root evaluation success is real and replayed.
It does not demonstrate reliable mastery: all final root evaluations still returned
zero improvement. These measurements argue against interpreting the failure simply
as the network being unable to represent a useful edit.

## Zero task rewards still produce policy updates

The implementation computes GAE using the critic, then centers and standardizes the
advantages before PPO. With inaccurate nonzero values, zero sampled rewards do not
imply zero advantages. We split the first-rollout GAE linearly into a reward term and
a critic term, and differentiate their centered contributions using the same original
advantage standard deviation. The derivatives are measured at the initial policy,
ratio one, before clipping or Adam, over the complete 1,024-example collection.
They are not a reconstruction of every later minibatch update.

For root-serial seed 29, every reward in the reconstructed first rollout is zero:

- Raw advantage standard deviation: 0.002062; standardization rescales it by about 485.
- Reward-derived policy gradient norm: exactly 0.
- Critic-derived policy gradient norm: 0.002959.
- Entropy-loss gradient norm, including its configured coefficient: 0.000000106.
- Value-loss gradient norm: 0.001716; this also updates shared representation weights.

The critic-derived gradient is nonzero in the action scorer itself, so the observation
is not explained solely by value-head updates. All five reconstructed reward-free
initial collections show this pattern. Logged mean approximate KL across whole runs
is about 0.0096–0.0124 despite most runs observing no task reward.

This is standard GAE/PPO behavior, not evidence of a sign or transition bug. GAE trades
variance against bias from value estimation; an inaccurate critic can supply a noisy
learning direction when sampled task feedback is absent. The decomposition identifies
the source of the initial update, not the fraction of final failure attributable to it.
See the primary [GAE paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.02438).

There is also contrary evidence to the stronger claim that critic effects always
swamp genuine rewards. In curriculum-serial seed 29's first collection, the single
positive reward produces a policy-gradient norm of 0.003804, versus 0.001802 from the
critic term. Genuine feedback can influence the policy when it occurs. In that
collection, however, the reward contributes credit to only one of 1,024 sampled steps:
the successful first action after the externally prepared prefix.

## What the evidence supports changing next

The fixed 8,192-edit advancement rule was a limitation of our experiment design.
The reverse-curriculum literature adapts start-state difficulty to observed performance;
our fixed schedule did not implement that property. See the primary
[reverse curriculum paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.05300).

The next controlled experiment should first establish reliable remaining-one behavior:
compare shorter curriculum episodes against 128, keep sampled-action and reset counts
explicit, and measure the successful bind's probability plus easy-start success rate.
Advance only after a predeclared mastery criterion, while continuing to evaluate the
original root. This requires neither behavior cloning nor supervised action labels.
A separate ablation could test zero initialization of the value output against the
current initialization, to isolate initial reward-free actor drift. Neither change
was applied during this diagnosis, and neither is claimed to guarantee improvement.

A longer horizon can be tested separately if traces actually show promising partial
solutions reaching the time limit. Multiplying both horizon and total samples would
confound the effects of longer episodes and more compute. A smaller episode horizon
in a curriculum also changes the training task and must be explicit; original-root
128-step evaluation should remain the common outcome measure.

## Evidence and reproduction

The [compact provenance manifest](2026-09-08.14-curriculum-failure-provenance.json)
links the original training manifest, diagnostic helper and retained JSON. Raw output
is ignored under `artifacts/factory-edit-pilot/curriculum-failure-diagnosis/`. The helper
was executed on the server from an artifact directory, with unchanged trained models
and native engine. Its CLI completed, all reconstruction checks passed, and every
next-action test restored the same post-first-action state before trying a candidate.

```sh
PYTHONPATH=tools/factory-edit-pilot tools/factory-edit-pilot/.venv/bin/python tools/factory-edit-pilot/curriculum_diagnosis.py --root "$PWD" --matrix artifacts/factory-edit-pilot/curriculum-parallel-learning --native-library target/release/libaic_pilot_native.so --output artifacts/factory-edit-pilot/fresh-curriculum-failure-diagnosis
```

The script reads trusted local PyTorch checkpoints. No optimizer update is performed.
Its data includes all policy probes, first-rollout value/advantage decomposition,
gradient components and individual first-action reachability results. Full causal
ranking of initialization, normalization, horizon and curriculum advancement remains
unresolved until those factors are ablated in separate controlled training runs.
