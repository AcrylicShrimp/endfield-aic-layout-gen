# Retained advantage signal results

Status: complete retrospective analysis of the same sixteen gradient cells, both
first-pair rates and all sixteen cases at updates 9–16. No new actions, training
or outcome sampling. GAE agrees with an independent finite residual sum; the large
rehearsal contribution is not evidence of an arithmetic defect. Its signal includes
both post-improvement baseline discrepancies and useful additional improvements.

## Arithmetic and scope

Each rate contributes 512 saved sixteen-command episodes, 8192 observations:
6144 current-stage and 2048 rehearsal observations. The saved configuration uses
`gamma = 1` and `gae_lambda = 0.95`. Independent GAE error is at most 7.400e-9.
Reconstructed normalized-advantage means agree with the incoming gradient
measurement's policy loss to within 3.264e-6, including its numerical forward error.
Normalization remains global per 1024-observation update, exactly as in training.

Sampled return-to-go is the sum of the actual remaining rewards in that finite
episode. It is not the true expected value of the state, a counterfactual better
action's value, or a ground-truth advantage. A successful action may legitimately
have negative estimated advantage when the baseline expects more. Neither this
comparison nor a small direct critic gradient proves or excludes critic bias.

## Where the signal occurs

Rehearsal supplies 25% of observations, but 27.67% of absolute normalized-GAE mass
at ordinary rate and 43.88% at lower rate. Absolute advantage mass measures sample
weight, not gradient magnitude or direction. Below, entries are ordinary/lower;
percentages divide by all absolute normalized-GAE mass in that rate's eight updates.

| current / first reward | 284/371 | 3.95/4.23 |
| current / after first reward | 1862/2492 | 8.16/11.25 |
| current / no positive episode | 1600/208 | 27.23/9.10 |
| rehearsal / before first reward | 386/449 | 5.67/8.11 |
| rehearsal / first reward | 116/126 | 1.93/2.93 |
| rehearsal / after first reward | 1354/1441 | 16.99/29.95 |
| rehearsal / no positive episode | 192/32 | 3.09/2.88 |

Current-stage episodes with no gain account for 1600 observations at ordinary rate
but only 208 at lower rate. Their advantage-mass share falls from 27.23% to 9.10%.
Consequently the relative rehearsal share also reflects successful current-stage
learning; disproportionate rehearsal weight alone cannot diagnose a harmful update.

After-first-reward rehearsal observations account for 29.95% of all lower-rate
absolute advantage mass, versus 16.99% at ordinary rate. Their mean predicted value
and sampled return-to-go are 0.03361/0.02874 (lower) and 0.01937/0.01868 (ordinary).
Aggregates hide different cases: lower-rate glass gap0 turn0 has mean predicted
value 0.03070 and zero sampled future reward in this phase; battery gap0 turn0 has
0.02426 and zero. But glass gap4 turn0 has prediction 0.06422 and sampled return
0.09333, showing the opposite discrepancy. These are conditional saved-trajectory
means, not evidence that all rehearsal values are systematically too high.

There are **24 ordinary-rate and 40 lower-rate positive reward actions after the
first improvement**, all in rehearsal episodes in this interval. They contribute
summed additional normalized area rewards 2.73623 and 4.49855. Current-stage episodes
have no later positive rewards here, but that does not justify imposing such a
restriction on the objective. Truncating every episode at first success would remove
real additional improvements and change the requested iterative improvement problem.

Replacing the finite-sum GAE diagnostic with sampled return minus saved value changes
raw advantage signs in 223/8192 observations (ordinary) and 393/8192 (lower).
After separately applying the same normalization formula, signs differ in 352/8192
and 430/8192. Sample Monte Carlo is another estimator, not an oracle; these numbers
do not establish that lambda one is superior or select a new estimator.

## Next controlled intervention

The stored stage schedule supplies one rehearsal episode per case per update.
Fourteen cases visit the **same** previous stage together: levels 1 through 8 at
updates 9 through 16. The other two cases, still at current level eight, visit
6, 7, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6. Thus the existing 3:1 mixture does not spread each case's
previous levels within an update, and its stage composition changes in a strongly
synchronized way. This is deliberate scheduling behavior, not a newly found bug.

A bounded next comparison can stagger rehearsal order across cases while preserving
each case's exact stage-visit multiset, 3:1 current/rehearsal ratio, complete roots,
32-update command allowance, optimizer settings and all earlier failed criteria.
It tests stage-order composition rather than assuming that more or less rehearsal
is the repair. Its own prospective protocol and outcome criteria must precede
sampling. Do not infer success from gradient norms or from a selected repaired case.
No permanent scheduler, reward or advantage-estimator change is selected here.

Stages 4/5 and original 59-facility acceptance remain incomplete. The available
results establish a retention difficulty, not a fundamental impossibility.
