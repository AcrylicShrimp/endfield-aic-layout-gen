# Why the bounded RL pilot does not yet improve reliably

## Conclusion and scope

The endpoints in report 30 do not demonstrate sustained learned improvement. The
two initializations have different observed failure patterns: random initialization
changes its action distribution little, while imitation-warm undergoes large
reversals and ultimately almost stops selecting poses. Missing optimizer updates
are ruled out. Weak reward coverage of prepared factories and uncontrolled policy
movement are confirmed problems; their causal contributions to final evaluation
quality still require controlled training comparisons.

This read-only diagnosis performs no factory edits or optimizer steps. It examines
the audited training result, 117 policy versions and ten fixed first-action
observations, yielding 1170 policy/observation measurements. The observations cover
all five original and five contraction-exhausted training resets. Reconstructed
observation identities and history masks match retained evidence. Every policy
remains unchanged. These measurements are not new rollout evaluations.

## Reward coverage is uneven

Only completed update groups enter these counts; the three unapplied warm episodes
are excluded.

| Initialization and reset | Groups | Nonzero reward-gradient groups | Positive-return episodes | Positive episodes whose incumbent improvements are all contractions |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Random original | 32 | 32 | 64 | 31 |
| Random prepared | 32 | 29 | 48 | 10 |
| Warm original | 25 | 25 | 49 | 35 |
| Warm prepared | 26 | 5 | 9 | 1 |

Warm prepared factories have zero reward gradient in 21 of 26 groups (80.8%).
The learner receives much less task-reward evidence at the starts intended to
require rearrangement. All 25 warm original groups have a reward difference, and
35 of their 49 positive-return episodes improve their incumbent only through
contraction actions. This describes observed incumbent improvements, not the causal
contribution of earlier exploratory actions. Eight of the nine positive prepared
episodes include an improving pose; useful pose outcomes exist but are uncommon.

Random training has nonzero reward gradients in 61 of 64 groups. An entirely
absent return signal therefore does not explain that arm. It records 554 immediate
incumbent improvements and 2088 executor rejections in 8192 actions. Its shared
policy still has only 24–28 episodes per reset case. Episodic relative returns
credit all sampled actions and use only three other terminal results as references.
Variance and exposure are plausible limitations, not established causal diagnoses.
The episodic gradient is not invalid simply because credit is shared over actions.

## The warm policy changes sharply without current task reward

For each fixed input, compare the complete action probability vectors. Total
variation (TV) is half their absolute difference sum: it measures redistributed
probability mass, not a score improvement or confidence interval. Means below
weight the ten fixed factory inputs equally.

- The first warm update changes the distribution by mean TV **0.134149**, despite
  zero reward gradient. The initial Adam state is empty, so this first transition
  comes from entropy regularization, not inherited reward momentum.
- The largest warm one-update change is **0.256819** at update 39, after zero-based
  group 38 on prepared Medical 48. That group also has zero reward gradient. This
  later transition combines entropy with inherited Adam moments; it cannot be
  attributed to entropy alone from this observation.
- Random initialization's largest one-update mean TV is **0.005168**. Its final
  distribution is only **0.052173** from initialization on this panel. Small policy
  movement does not prove its direction is useful or that more data will suffice.

The warm first-step reward-gradient norm is exactly zero; its entropy-gradient
norm is 0.022061, below the 0.5 clipping threshold. Yet its median absolute parameter
change is **0.000293169**, close to Adam's learning rate **0.0003**. About **63.98%**
of its 39745 parameters move by more than 90% of that learning-rate magnitude.
Reconstructing the step from saved bias-corrected Adam moments matches recorded
parameter changes within **5.82e-8** maximum absolute error, consistent with
float32 rounding.

For this first step, Adam divides the first moment by the square root of the
second moment plus epsilon. Scaling a non-negligible gradient scales both terms;
an entropy coefficient of 0.001 therefore does not imply a parameter step one
thousandth as large. The small coefficient was insufficient protection against
large policy movement here. Gradient-norm clipping before Adam is not a bound on
action-distribution change. This is a verified optimization mechanism, not evidence
that entropy regularization or Adam is universally unsuitable.

## The reversal appears on unchanged inputs

| Warm policy snapshot | Mean pose probability: five originals | Mean pose probability: five prepared resets |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Initialization | 17.135% | 2.271% |
| After 16 updates | 56.314% | 48.332% |
| After 32 updates | 79.999% | 80.002% |
| After 48 updates | Below 0.000002% | 0.014813% |
| Terminal, 51 updates | Below 0.000001% | 0.000506% |

At update 32, pose probability exceeds 90% on eight of the ten panel states.
It subsequently reverses. On original resets, terminal mean contraction
probability is 98.058%; prepared resets instead put 73.650% on insertion and
26.350% on restore-best because direct contraction is absent at those initial
states. This confirms that the endpoint's reduced pose selection is actual policy
drift, rather than merely a different rollout seed or changed factory input.

Report 30's held-fixed endpoint rollout comparison remains the behavioral evidence:
warm pose selections fall from 35 to 2, with no improving terminal pose or reroute;
both initializations have 12 wins, 12 ties and 11 losses against their own initial
policy. The panel cannot show that the pose-heavy intermediate policy was better;
no favorable intermediate checkpoint is substituted for the registered endpoint.

Initial-to-terminal parameter L2 changes are 3.627% of the initial norm for random
and 2.896% for warm. Similar parameter-change magnitudes produce very different
distribution movement. Parameter norms alone are therefore poor outcome monitors.

## Next decision and unresolved causes

The initial suggestion that the warm learner simply favors easy contraction
throughout training was incomplete: the full panel exposes an intervening
pose-heavy phase and a later reversal. The strongest next diagnostic is to isolate
entropy and inherited optimizer effects at the recorded flat-return transitions,
then register one-variable controls for flat-return update handling and actual
update size. Preserve the original task-score priority and valid incumbents.
Any multi-step training branch must collect fresh on-policy experience after its
parameters diverge; old traces cannot be reused as fresh on-policy gradients.

More experience may still be needed, particularly for random initialization. The
fixed 32-action horizon, four-episode relative-return variance, shared-policy
interference between reset types and functional step size remain competing or
interacting explanations. No ablation has established their separate effect on
final factory quality. Parallel collection increases throughput; it does not by
itself correct these learning dynamics. Its timed matrix was not launched while
answering the user's cause question.
