# Curriculum and concurrent PPO results

Status: complete. All 12 runs and independent audits passed; matrix wall time was
854.88 seconds. Concurrent collection reduced mean full training time by 41–42%.
Curriculum produced 15 positive training episodes from privileged easy starts, but
all final original-root evaluations remained at zero improvement. One intermediate
original-root success was independently reproduced and did not persist to the final
checkpoint. No supervised pretraining or reward shaping was used.

## Question and controlled implementation

The previous two-case study found no positive sampled rewards. This study applies the
user-approved curriculum and parallel collection ideas while excluding behavior
cloning, supervised losses, demonstration replay and reward shaping. The native
edit domain, exact acceptance and normalized retained-best area reward are unchanged.
The [contract](../designs/2026-09-08.06-curriculum-parallel-ppo.md) and
[protocol](../../tools/factory-edit-pilot/curriculum-parallel-study.json) specify four
conditions and three independent training seeds (17, 29, 43).

Every condition receives 65,536 sampled actions, split equally between A and B. A PPO
update consumes eight complete 128-action episodes (1,024 samples), with four epochs
and 64-example minibatches. This matches update cadence across conditions; the earlier
256-sample study is historical context. Each model starts from the same weights within
its training seed. The serial collector uses CPU inference; the concurrent collector
uses eight independent native engines, a persistent thread pool, batched GPU inference
and independent CPU sampling generators. Native observation/transition work releases
the GIL. Complete episodes are flattened by stream, preserving terminal GAE boundaries.

Curriculum starts are existing zero-reward prefixes before each case's first known
improvement. A's witness needs six actions; B's needs three. Leaving one, two or three
actions gives A prefixes of 5/4/3 actions and B prefixes of 2/1/0 actions. These are
sufficient suffix lengths, not proofs of minimum edit distance. The first 8,192 sampled
actions use remaining-one starts, the next 8,192 remaining-two, the next 8,192
remaining-three, and the last 40,960 use original roots. Each stage splits evenly by
case. The native API verifies every setup action against its actual domain, rejects
rewarded or completed-changing prefixes, preserves original valid base/best and resets
the episode clock. Failed admission preserves prior state and invalidates stale handles.

This is privileged selection of starting states. Prefix commands are never action
labels or PPO samples. Setup consumes 480 extra native actions per curriculum run;
its time is included in training time and its count is separate from sampled exposure.
Training gains from those starts must not be presented as gains discovered from a root.
Every frozen evaluation uses the original root, 128 actions and 32 fixed sampling
seeds per case. Evaluation runs on CPU and cannot advance training sampling RNGs.

## Fixed-action collection benchmark

The server uses an Intel Core Ultra 9 285K and NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5090 (driver
610.57.04), with 16 PyTorch CPU threads and a float32 CUDA learner; TF32 is disabled.
It replays the same 1,024 action indices in each trial. Real policy inference
and sampling execute, but sampled actions are discarded for this timing diagnostic.
Final geometry, retained best, score, step count and episode counters must match.
Each mode has five measured repeats plus warm-up, with rotated execution order.
Timing includes observation, inference/sampling and native transitions; setup and
verification are excluded. The full study separately includes preparation overhead.

| Start | CPU serial | GPU batch, serial native | GPU batch, threaded native |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Original roots | 0.5254 s | 0.2492 s | 0.2175 s |
| One known action remaining | 0.5305 s | 0.2561 s | 0.2202 s |

These median measurements show about 2.41x collection throughput versus CPU serial.
GPU batching accounts for most of the gain; threading reduces the already batched
collection time by a further 12.7–14.0%. This is an eight-environment implementation,
not an eightfold speedup, and this replay does not measure learning quality.

## Frozen training and original-root outcomes

The completed matrix used 786,432 sampled
training actions, 6,144 episodes, 768 PPO updates and 49,152 optimizer steps, plus
2,880 curriculum setup actions. Six evaluations per run consume another 589,824
actions. Thirty-two evaluation seeds are repeated samples from each frozen policy;
the three trained models are the independent replicates.

| Condition | Positive training episodes by seed 17 / 29 / 43 | Mean training time | Final A mean reduction | Final B mean reduction |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Root, serial | 0 / 0 / 0 | 51.959 s | 0% | 0% |
| Curriculum, serial | 2 / 5 / 1 | 52.630 s | 0% | 0% |
| Root, concurrent | 0 / 0 / 0 | 30.640 s | 0% | 0% |
| Curriculum, concurrent | 0 / 6 / 1 | 30.720 s | 0% | 0% |

Training time excludes frozen evaluation, includes curriculum preparation and retained
training evidence, and uses the same PPO update budget. Paired concurrent reductions
were 34.3–45.3% for root starts and 36.8–45.7% for curriculum starts. Mean collection
time fell from 37.475 to 16.979 seconds for root starts and from 37.897 to 16.877 seconds
for curriculum starts. Update time remains part of the total, so collection speedup
does not translate directly into the same full-training speedup.

All 15 positive training episodes occurred at remaining-one starts (eight A, seven B).
There were no positive training episodes at remaining-two, remaining-three, or after
returning to original roots. The best curriculum training scores were A `[140,21,0]`
and B `[363,48,2]`; these include privileged setup and are not original-root policy
outcomes. All selected training champions retained original facility placements.
No evaluation had multiple positive improvements.

Every final policy had zero mean, median and maximum area reduction on both cases;
the seed ranges were also zero. Across all checkpoints there was one positive
original-root evaluation: curriculum-serial, training seed 43, checkpoint 32,768,
evaluation seed 100027, case A. It returned `[147,24,0]`, improving area 154 to 147
(4.545%). That checkpoint's A mean was 0.1420% over 32 evaluation rollouts, with a
3.125% success frequency. Averaged across the three trained policies, the A mean at
that checkpoint was 0.04735%. At 65,536 it returned to zero. All B evaluations and
all other condition/checkpoint evaluations were zero.

The positive event was reproduced from the saved CPU evaluation policy and exact
sampling seed, matching return, score and episode counters. The independent Python
reference replayed every one of its 128 native transitions and final retained geometry.
Starting from the original root, it unbound `battery:target`, extended the outlet one
cell west, erased the old and newly extended low cells, then rebound at `[2,-1]`.
The fifth action completed the improved factory. This is a valid isolated success,
not evidence of reliable transfer or a reason to select a favorable checkpoint after
examining all results.

![Original-root evaluation means and training-seed ranges](../../artifacts/factory-edit-pilot/curriculum-parallel-learning/comparison/learning-comparison.png)

The sampling bottleneck remains visible even in the easiest starts: A has 37 candidate
actions and B 87, each with one known immediate-success bind. Each run supplies only
32 remaining-one episodes per case; across six curriculum runs, just 15 of the 384
easiest episodes reached positive reward. The fixed schedule advanced regardless of
that frequency. This is evidence that this particular curriculum budget did not
establish reliable prerequisite behavior. It does not establish that curriculum
learning generally fails. Any follow-up should separately test advancement based on
measured easy-start mastery, rather than silently changing this completed protocol.

## Interpretation limits

Only two already seen factory roots are tested. The curriculum contains a small fixed
set of privileged starts, and reaching reward from those starts alone is not transfer
to the original task. Three seeds cannot establish universal algorithm rankings.
The parallel treatment changes CPU scalar inference to GPU batching as well as native
threading; the fixed replay isolates the threading contribution, while full learning
trajectories may differ numerically and stochastically. The reward remains area-only,
with retained best selection using the existing lexicographic score. No global
optimality, infeasibility or unseen-factory generalization is established.
